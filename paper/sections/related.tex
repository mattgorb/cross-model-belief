\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Correlated errors undermine oversight --- at the output level.}
The claim that models fail together, and that this is a problem for oversight
built on agreement, is established and is not ours.
\citet{goel2025alike} introduce a chance-adjusted measure of model similarity
based on overlap in mistakes, and show that judges favour models similar to
themselves, that weak-to-strong training works better when supervisor and student
have complementary rather than overlapping knowledge, and that mistakes grow more
similar as capability grows. \citet{kim2025correlated} measure error correlation
across more than $350$ models and find models agreeing on the wrong answer around
$60\%$ of the time when both err, with the correlation \emph{rising} with
accuracy and persisting across architectures and providers.
\citet{ding2026agree} audit agreement itself as a confidence signal and find it
unreliable in the same direction.

Our measurement is the internal-state counterpart of theirs. Every one of these
works reads outputs, so none of them can distinguish two models reaching the same
wrong answer by the same internal route from two reaching it by different routes
--- which is exactly the distinction that decides whether a probe-based checker
escapes the problem. Our chance-adjusted quantity, the error correlation
normalized by the maximum its marginals permit, is the same \emph{kind} of
statistic as their chance-adjusted agreement; we derive it from the exact
decomposition of the false-agreement rate rather than defining it directly, which
is what lets us say why family diversity fails to help.

\paragraph{Probes, truth directions, and their failure modes.}
\citet{burns2023ccs} recover a truth direction without supervision, and
\citet{marks2024geometry} show the direction is linear and causally relevant.
\citet{liu2023dissonance} examine where probes and outputs disagree and find
probes often the more accurate of the two, which is the premise a probe-based
checker needs. The method's limits are equally well documented:
\citet{farquhar2024challenges} show unsupervised discovery can lock onto
whichever salient binary feature the data offers rather than truth. We reproduce
that failure --- our unsupervised probe reads truth on curated factual statements
and not on adversarial misconceptions --- and it is why the probes we rely on are
supervised.

Closest in spirit to our question, \citet{pandey2026sycophancy} find a small set
of attention heads carrying a ``this statement is wrong'' signal across twelve
open-weight models from five labs, so a shared internal signal across models is
not only plausible but observed. They study whether a model notices its own
error; we study whether two models' readouts fail together, and whether that
joint failure is detectable.

\paragraph{Transporting probes between models.}
Reusing a probe characterized on one model requires a map between activation
spaces. \citet{chen2025stitching} fit affine maps between residual streams and
transfer sparse autoencoders, probes and steering vectors across model sizes;
\citet{oozeer2025interventions} transfer safety interventions through learned
mappings across Llama, Qwen and Gemma. The counter-evidence matters too:
\citet{zhang2026negative} report that a map can align representations well under
an offline objective while the translated states remain unusable causally inside
the receiver. Our Gate~B measures what transfer costs a probe in the only unit
that matters for oversight --- discriminative performance lost --- and we report
it per dataset rather than as a single verdict, because it ranges from almost
nothing to total failure.

\paragraph{Representational similarity, and why we do not lean on it.}
CKA \citep{kornblith2019similarity} is the standard measure of whether two
networks represent inputs alike, and the platonic representation hypothesis
\citep{huh2024platonic} predicts convergence with scale and capability --- which,
if it held strongly, would predict exactly the correlated failures above. We
report CKA as context but not as the verdict on transferability, because a probe
needs one direction to survive while CKA grades all of them: we observe a pair at
CKA $0.49$ whose transported probe loses $0.004$ AUROC. CKA does predict transfer
loss moderately across our cells, which we report, but it is the wrong instrument
for the question.

\paragraph{Scalable oversight.}
Debate \citep{irving2018debate}, sandwiching \citep{bowman2022measuring} and
weak-to-strong training \citep{burns2023weak} all place models in the checking
role, and \citet{panickssery2024selfpreference} show judges recognize and favour
their own generations, one concrete mechanism by which a checker's errors
correlate with the checked model's. On the theoretical side,
\citet{afrin2026ensembles} analyse evaluator ensembles under optimization
pressure and prove that common-mode error is not identifiable from the judges'
own scores --- disagreement reveals only the orthogonal component. Our
empirical finding, that the shared blind spot is barely detectable from the
activations that produced it, is the representation-level analogue of that
result, reached from the other direction.

\paragraph{Ensemble diversity.}
The dependence of ensemble error on the covariance between members is classical
\citep{brown2005diversity}, and the correlation floor we state for $N$ overseers
is a restatement of it rather than a new result. The constraint that a joint
distribution's marginals impose on how much two binary errors can overlap is
older still \citep{frechet1951tableaux}. Our use of both is to turn them on
probe-based oversight and to report where the measured quantities sit inside the
bounds they imply.
