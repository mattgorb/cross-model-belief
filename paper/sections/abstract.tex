\begin{abstract}
Oversight of one language model by another assumes the two fail differently, and
a checker that reads only outputs cannot test that assumption on anything the
model leaves unsaid. We ask it of internal state instead: we fit a linear belief
probe on each of two models, read both on the same claim, and measure
\emph{false agreement}, where both probes call a false claim true. This is the
case disagreement-based oversight cannot see.
%
On the false claims the rate decomposes exactly as
$\mathrm{FA} = p_1 p_2 + \rho\sqrt{p_1(1-p_1)}\sqrt{p_2(1-p_2)}$, into a term
set by the two probes' accuracies and a term set by the correlation between
their mistakes, and Fr\'echet bounds confine it to
$\max(0,\,p_1+p_2-1) \le \mathrm{FA} \le \min(p_1,p_2)$. False agreement can
therefore never fall below the better probe's error rate, and once the two
probes are jointly inaccurate enough, overlap is forced even at zero
correlation.
%
Measuring this across $571$ model-pair--dataset cells --- $16$ models, five
families, $1.7$B to $405$B parameters, five datasets, two probe families --- we
find false agreement on $2$--$26\%$ of false claims, with positively correlated
probe errors in $84$--$98\%$ of cells depending on the probe. Choosing a checker
from a different family does not reduce it: $12.5\%$ within family against
$13.2\%$ across for a logistic probe and $11.7\%$ against $11.2\%$ for a
mass-mean probe, neither distinguishable from zero ($|t| = 0.59$ in both), with
the sign of the difference reversing between probes.
%
Cross-family pairs do show lower error correlation in aggregate
($\rho = 0.42 \to 0.34$, $t = -3.7$), but the effect disappears once model size
is matched ($0.389$ against $0.406$ at a size ratio below $1.5$), so the
decorrelation usually credited to architectural diversity is a size effect
wearing a family label. The identity explains why even the aggregate reduction
cannot help: $\rho$ enters false agreement damped by $p(1-p)\approx 0.19$,
while the accuracy cost of a diverse pairing enters the undamped term $p_1p_2$.
%
Finally, the blind spot is only partly legible in the activations that produce
it. A direction fitted on the false agreements of four datasets transfers to a
fifth, but works on factual recall (AUROC $0.92$) and not on entailment or
open-ended question answering ($0.54$--$0.56$); it catches $42\%$ of false
claims at a $10\%$ review budget, of which the probes' own disagreement already
supplies about half.
%
The limit on this kind of oversight is shared error. It is not removed by
choosing a checker from a different family, and it tightens as probes become
more accurate, because accuracy enters only the term that diversity cannot move.
\end{abstract}
