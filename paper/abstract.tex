\begin{abstract}
Overseeing a language model with another language model rests on an assumption
that is almost never tested: that the two fail differently. The overseer reads
outputs, so anything the model does not express escapes it, and any error the two
share escapes it as well.

Linear probes offer a way past the first problem. They recover a truth direction
from activations, giving direct access to what a model represents rather than
what it reports. We ask whether they escape the second.

Fitting probes on two models and comparing their readouts, we characterize
\emph{false agreement}, the case where both probes read a claim as true and the
claim is false, and show its rate depends on the correlation between the probes'
errors rather than their individual accuracies.

We measure this across model pairs varying in family and scale, transporting
probes with a linear alignment map and reporting the cost of transport
explicitly.
